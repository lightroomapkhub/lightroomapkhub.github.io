Exposure Triangle Explainer

Aperture, shutter speed and ISO work together to control exposure. Move the sliders and pick a scene — the meter shows whether your photo would be under-exposed, correct or over-exposed, and the preview changes to match.

How it works: every full stop of aperture, shutter or ISO doubles or halves the light. A correct exposure matches the scene's brightness. Opening the aperture (smaller f-number) or slowing the shutter lets in more light, but changes depth of field and motion blur — that is the trade-off at the heart of photography. Lightroom's exposure slider fixes small mistakes afterwards, but it cannot recover a badly over-exposed sky or a very noisy dark photo.
