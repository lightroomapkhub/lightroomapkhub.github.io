Best Lightroom Settings for Instagram Photos

Start With the Right Crop and Aspect Ratio

Instagram shows photos in a few fixed shapes, so crop before you edit, not after. The feed displays portrait photos at 4:5, which fills the most screen space and is the best choice for portraits, travel shots and anything with a clear subject. Square 1:1 works well for a classic grid look, and landscape 1.91:1 suits wide scenes, though it appears small in the feed. Stories and Reels use the full 9:16 vertical frame. Pick the ratio that fits your subject, straighten the horizon, and remove distracting edges early. A photo cropped after editing often needs its exposure and composition rebalanced, so settle the frame first. One more habit: leave a little breathing room around faces and text, because Instagram's interface can cover the edges of a photo in some views.

Exposure and Contrast That Survive Compression

Instagram compresses every upload, which softens detail and can crush dark areas, so your exposure needs to be right before the file leaves Lightroom. Aim for a balanced histogram: no tall spike at either edge, and detail visible in both shadows and highlights. A good starting point is exposure around 0 to +0.30 for a slightly bright, feed-friendly look, highlights pulled down to about -20 to -40 to recover sky detail, and shadows lifted to about +20 to +40 so dark areas do not turn into black blocks. Add contrast of +10 to +20 for punch, but avoid going much further, because heavy contrast plus compression makes skin look harsh. If a photo is underexposed by more than a stop, brightening it in editing adds noise that compression makes worse.

Colour: White Balance, Vibrance and HSL

Colour is what makes a feed feel consistent, and white balance is where it starts. Use the eyedropper on something neutral, like a white wall, or set temperature by eye until skin looks natural. Then add vibrance of +10 to +25. Vibrance is safer than saturation because it boosts dull colours while protecting skin tones, which is why it is the better slider for portraits. Use saturation sparingly, usually no more than +10, and often leave it at zero. For finer control, open the HSL panel: lower orange saturation slightly to calm skin, raise blue saturation a touch for skies, and shift green hue a little towards teal for a popular travel look. Keep these changes small. A feed where every photo has the same gentle colour treatment looks far more professional than one where each photo has a different heavy filter.

Sharpening, Noise and Detail

Sharpening is the setting most people overdo for Instagram. The platform softens photos when it resizes them, so a little sharpening helps, but too much creates crunchy edges and halos. A sensible range is sharpening of 25 to 40, with radius at 1.0 and detail around 25. Hold the masking slider high, around 60 to 80, so sharpening applies to edges rather than smooth areas like sky and skin. For night or indoor photos, add luminance noise reduction of 20 to 40 to smooth grain, and a little colour noise reduction to remove speckles. Avoid clarity above +20 for portraits, because it emphasises skin texture. Texture is gentler than clarity and works well at +10 to +20 for landscapes and food. The test is simple: zoom to 100 percent and look at the edges. If they look harsh, you have gone too far.

Tip: Save your finished Instagram look as a preset named for your style, such as Feed Bright or Feed Warm. Apply it to new photos, then adjust only exposure and white balance per photo. That is how consistent feeds are built.

Export Settings for a Sharp Feed

Export is where many good edits lose quality, so get it right. Instagram displays feed photos at 1080 pixels wide, so export at 1080 pixels on the long edge for feed posts. Exporting much larger files does not help, because Instagram resizes them anyway, and exporting smaller files guarantees softness. Use JPEG format at quality 85 to 95, and use the sRGB colour space, since that is what phones and browsers expect. If your photo is a portrait at 4:5, export at 1080 by 1350 pixels. For Stories and Reels, export at 1080 by 1920. Turn on output sharpening for screen at a standard amount if your export panel offers it. Finally, upload from your phone over a stable connection and avoid re-saving the JPEG in another app first, because every extra save adds compression and softness.

Keeping a Consistent Look

A great feed is not one great photo, it is twenty photos that belong together. Pick one editing style and stay with it: the same white balance tendency, the same vibrance range, the same contrast level. Build one preset from your best edit and use it as the starting point for every photo, adjusting only exposure and white balance to suit each shot. Before posting, check your grid: look at the last nine photos and ask whether the new one fits or clashes. If it clashes, tone the edit down rather than pushing it further. Consistency also means restraint. The settings in this guide are starting points, not rules, and the best Instagram photos are usually the ones edited least. Get the light and colour right, export cleanly, and let the photo speak.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size should I export for Instagram?

Export feed photos at 1080 pixels wide, or 1080 by 1350 for 4:5 portraits. Stories and Reels should be 1080 by 1920 pixels.

Why do my photos look dull after uploading to Instagram?

Instagram compresses uploads, which softens detail and dulls colour. Exporting at the right size, using sRGB and adding a little sharpening before upload usually fixes it.

Should I use the same preset for every photo?

Use one preset as a starting point for consistency, but adjust exposure and white balance for each photo. A preset is a base, not a finished edit.
