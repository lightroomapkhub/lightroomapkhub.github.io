Best Lightroom Presets in 2026

What Makes a Preset Worth Using in 2026

A preset is a saved set of editing adjustments that you can apply to a photo with one tap. The best presets in 2026 share the same qualities: they are gentle, they work on many different photos, and they leave room for you to adjust. A good preset changes the mood of an image without destroying detail, pushing skin tones orange, or crushing the shadows into black. It should feel like a starting point, not a finished edit. When you test a preset, try it on five very different photos: a portrait in shade, a sunny landscape, an indoor shot, a night photo and a backlit scene. If it only looks good on one of them, it is too narrow.

Best Presets for Portraits

Portrait presets should protect skin above everything else. Look for packs described as clean, natural, warm or soft light, and check the before and after samples for even skin tones. The best portrait presets in 2026 lift the shadows slightly, soften the highlights on the face, and add a touch of warmth without turning skin yellow. They usually keep the background a little muted so the person stands out. A good test is to apply the preset and zoom in on the face: if the skin looks smooth but still has texture, the preset is doing its job. If the skin looks plastic or grey, move on.

Best Presets for Travel and Landscapes

Travel and landscape presets need to handle big skies, mixed light and strong colour without making the scene look fake. The most useful ones boost the blues and greens gently, add a little clarity to bring out texture in mountains and buildings, and keep the highlights under control so clouds keep their shape. Warm sunset presets are popular, but the best ones warm only the highlights and leave the shadows cooler, which keeps the image from looking like a single orange filter. Avoid landscape presets that push saturation to the maximum. They look exciting on a phone screen for a few seconds, but they print badly and tire the eye quickly. A preset that makes a dull grey day look slightly better is far more valuable than one that only works on an already perfect sunset.

Best Presets for Food and Product Shots

Food and product photography has one rule: the colours must stay honest. A burger that looks purple or a white plate that turns blue will ruin the shot, no matter how stylish the preset claims to be. The best food presets add warmth and a little contrast, brighten the whites, and keep reds and oranges rich without letting them bleed into everything else. Look for packs made specifically for food, cafes or product photography, and test them on a photo with a white background first. If the white stays white, the preset is safe. Bright, clean presets work well for menus and online shops, while moody, darker presets suit coffee shops and restaurants with warm lighting. For product shots, a preset with slightly lifted shadows helps small details show up on a phone screen, which is where most customers will see the image first.

Film Looks That Still Hold Up

Film-inspired presets remain popular in 2026 because they give digital photos a softer, more timeless feel. The good ones mimic the gentle contrast curve, muted highlights and subtle grain of real film, rather than simply adding a heavy orange tint. Classic styles include a warm Kodak-style look with soft reds, a cool Fujifilm-style look with clean greens, and a faded vintage look with lifted blacks. When you use a film preset, reduce the grain if you plan to print the photo, and check the shadows: film looks often lift the blacks, which can make a photo look washed out on a bright screen. Used lightly, a film preset can make an ordinary snapshot feel like a memory.

How to Choose a Preset Pack and What to Avoid

Start with your most common subject and buy or download a small pack made for it, rather than a giant bundle of hundreds of presets you will never open. Check that the pack is made for Lightroom Mobile, read real reviews, and look at before and after examples on ordinary photos, not just the seller's best shots. Free presets from reputable photographers and well-known editing blogs are a safe way to learn what you like. Be careful with preset packs bundled with modified or cracked versions of Lightroom. Those files often come from untrusted sources and can carry malware, steal account details, or install unwanted software on your phone. They also stop receiving updates, so they break when the official app changes. The safest route is the official Lightroom app from your phone's app store, with presets from trusted creators. A preset is only a tool: the best results still come from a well-exposed photo and a few minutes of careful adjustment after you apply it.

Stay safe: Never install a modified Lightroom APK from a random website to unlock premium presets. Modded APKs can contain malware, get your Adobe account banned, and receive no security updates. Use the official app and presets from trusted creators instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need paid presets to edit good photos?

No. Free presets from reputable creators and the built-in presets in Lightroom Mobile are enough to learn with, and many professionals use only a handful of presets they made themselves.

Why does the same preset look different on each photo?

Because every photo has different light, colour and exposure. A preset is a fixed set of adjustments, so it reacts differently to each image. Adjust the exposure and white balance after applying a preset.

How many presets should I keep?

Keep ten or fewer that you actually use. A small set you know well will give you faster, more consistent results than a library of hundreds you never learn.
