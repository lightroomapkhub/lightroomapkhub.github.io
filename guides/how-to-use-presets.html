How to Use Presets in Lightroom Mobile

How Presets Work in Lightroom Mobile

A preset in Lightroom Mobile is a saved recipe of editing settings: exposure, contrast, colour, sharpening and more, stored together so you can apply them all at once. When you tap a preset, Lightroom copies those saved slider values onto your photo. Nothing is permanent. Your original photo stays untouched, and you can undo the preset, change any slider afterwards, or remove it completely. Lightroom Mobile comes with a set of built-in presets grouped by style, such as Creative, Colour, Black and White and Vintage. You can also add presets from other photographers, usually shared as DNG files or as a preset pack you import.

Installing a Preset from a DNG File

Most presets shared online come as DNG files, which are really sample photos carrying the preset settings inside them. To install one, first save the DNG file to your phone, then open Lightroom Mobile and import it like any other photo. Open the DNG image, tap the three-dot menu in the top corner, and choose Create Preset. Give the preset a clear name, such as Warm Portrait, and pick a preset group to store it in, or create a new group for your collection. Tap the tick to save it, and the preset will now appear in the Presets panel for every photo you edit. Repeat the process for each DNG file in the pack. If you bought a preset pack, the seller usually includes a short guide with the same steps. Always get DNG preset files from a trusted creator or a reputable site. Random preset files passed around on file-sharing sites can be bundled with modified apps or suspicious links, so stick to sources you can verify.

Applying and Adjusting a Preset

To use a preset, open a photo in Lightroom Mobile and tap Presets at the bottom of the screen. Browse the groups, tap a preset, and watch the preview update. Swipe through a few options before deciding, because the first one is rarely the best fit. Once you apply a preset, do not stop there. The most important step is adjusting its strength to suit your photo. There is no single opacity slider for presets in Lightroom Mobile, so you adjust strength by changing the sliders the preset moved. If the effect is too strong, lower the contrast, pull back the saturation, or reduce the clarity a little. Almost always check the exposure and white balance after applying a preset, because these two settings vary the most between photos. A preset that looks perfect on a sunny photo may make a cloudy photo too dark or too blue. Tap and hold the photo to compare the before and after, and only keep the preset if the edited version is clearly better than the original.

Saving Your Own Preset

Once you edit a photo you love, save those settings as your own preset so you can reuse them. Edit the photo until you are happy with it, then tap the three-dot menu and choose Create Preset. Name it after what it is for, such as Golden Hour Portrait or Cafe Food, and save it into a group you will remember. One useful habit is to save a preset before you make the final small tweaks, so the preset stays flexible for other photos. You can also create variations: save a warmer version and a cooler version of the same edit, and you will have a small personal pack that matches your style. Your own presets have a big advantage over bought ones, because they were made on your photos, in your light, with your camera. Over time, most photographers find they use their own presets more than any pack they downloaded. Keep your collection small and delete presets you never use, so the list stays fast to browse.

Tip: Before saving your own preset, reset the crop, spot removal and local adjustments. A preset that carries a crop from one photo will crop every other photo the same way, which is rarely what you want.

Fixing Common Preset Problems

The most common preset problem is a photo that turns out too dark, too orange or too washed out. Start by checking exposure and white balance, since those two sliders fix most bad results. If the skin looks orange, go to the Colour panel, open the Mix, and pull the orange saturation down slightly while shifting the orange hue a little towards yellow. If the photo looks flat and grey, the preset probably lifted the blacks too far, so lower the blacks slider and add a touch of contrast. If the colours look unreal, reduce the vibrance rather than the saturation, because vibrance affects the muted colours first and leaves skin tones safer. When a preset makes a photo worse in every way, do not force it: that preset simply does not suit that photo, and a different one will. Delete presets that fail on most of your photos. A preset collection should make editing faster, not leave you fighting the sliders on every image.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Lightroom Mobile presets free?

Many are. Lightroom Mobile includes free built-in presets, and many photographers share free packs. Paid packs exist too, but free presets are enough to learn with.

Can I use the same preset on Android and iPhone?

Yes. Presets are saved to your Adobe account, so a preset you create or import on one device syncs to Lightroom on your other devices when you are signed in.

Why did my imported preset disappear?

This usually happens when the DNG file was deleted before the preset was saved, or when you are signed in with a different Adobe account. Re-import the DNG file and check which account you are using.
